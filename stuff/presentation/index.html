On 1 July 2024 India renumbered its criminal law.

No such section. The BNS ends at 358.

A section number means nothing without its code, and the offence date decides the code.

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita replaced the Indian Penal Code on 1 July 2024, and almost every offence got a new number. Offences committed before that date are still tried under the IPC, so for years every lawyer works with both. [click] 302 is murder in the IPC; in the BNS it is hurting religious feelings. [click] 303 was murder by a life-convict; in the BNS it is theft. [click] And 420, the best-known cheating section, does not exist in the BNS at all. [click] So a citation that is right in one code is a different crime, or nothing, in the other. That is exactly where AI tools go wrong.

Problem statement

Build a research assistant for Indian criminal law that identifies applicable sections and precedents across the IPC→BNS transition, never presents a non-existent citation, and runs fully offline.

The second problem: language models invent citations. This is the very model inside Pramana. Asked what BNS 303 covers, it confidently described a bomb-threat offence. [click] BNS 303 is theft. [click] When we withheld retrieval on our 15 development cases and let it answer from memory, 20 of its 68 citations named sections that do not exist in the BNS, mostly old IPC numbers. [click] And this is not only small models: a 2024 Stanford study found commercial legal AI research tools hallucinating in roughly 17 to 33 percent of queries. [click] So the problem statement is: build a research assistant for Indian criminal law that finds the applicable sections and precedents across the transition, never presents a non-existent citation, and runs fully offline. Research gap

No system combines offline operation, date-based code routing, an IPC→BNS precedent bridge, graph-verified citations and outcome statistics.

On the research side: retrieval-augmented generation grounds a model’s answer in retrieved text, but nothing in it checks that a citation exists. LeSICiN identifies statutes with a graph model, but it is supervised and IPC-only. IL-TUR is the Indian legal benchmark we evaluate on, with IPC-era labels. Magesh and colleagues measured hallucination in commercial tools but do not prevent it. BGE-M3 is a strong general embedder that had not been tested on Indian criminal law. On the tool side: Indian Kanoon and the paid databases are search engines; they return real documents but do not interpret facts. jhana.ai and NyayAssist do verify citations, so verification alone is not new. [click] The gap is the combination: offline operation for confidentiality, choosing the code by offence date, bridging new BNS charges to old IPC precedent, graph-verified citations, and outcome statistics. That gives us our objectives.

06 · Methodology · system architecture

The model proposes. The graph decides.

Deterministic code
Language model
Graph lookup
Built offline ChromaDB · 4 collections Citation graph · NetworkX Outcomes · SQLite
This is the architecture, and the key idea is in the title: the model proposes, the graph decides. Solid boxes are ordinary code; dashed boxes are the language model, used in only three places: proposing sections, judging whether they apply, and drafting arguments. Everything that decides whether a citation is real is code. A router picks the code from the date, retrieval finds candidates, a guardrail stops early if nothing is close, Node 1 proposes, Node 2 verifies against the graph and sends rejections back, Node 2b tests applicability, precedents and outcome statistics run in parallel, Node 4 drafts arguments, and a final check re-verifies everything new. [click] Three stores are built offline. The next slides walk through the pipeline stage by stage with the motorcycle case, starting with the data those stores are built from.

Why retrieval-augmented generation instead of fine-tuning a legal model? The law keeps changing, and with retrieval new judgments are simply added. Every candidate traces back to a real indexed section. And it fits a 4 GB GPU, because nothing is trained. A published Indian legal model that was fine-tuned actually scored below its own base model, which supports this choice. Here is the motorcycle case. [click] The date rule picks the BNS. [click] BGE-M3 turns the facts into a 1024-dimensional vector. [click] Two rankings are built: similarity to statute text, and votes from similar judgments for the sections they applied, combined by reciprocal rank fusion. [click] BNS 303, theft, comes first: third by text and second by votes. A guardrail checks that the closest judgment is near enough before the model is asked at all. The model then proposes from these candidates, and every proposal is verified.

This is the heart of the system. The citation graph holds every section of both codes, every judgment, and an edge whenever a judgment cites a section. Precedents come only from graph edges, so every precedent really did apply the charged section. [click] For BNS 303, not one judgment in our corpus applies it yet; BNS theft cases have not reached the higher courts in bulk. For objective two we used IL-TUR, the published Indian legal benchmark from ACL 2024. The best systems on it are trained on 42 thousand labelled documents and score 28.1 macro-F1. Pramana has no training on this task: it is a 4-billion-parameter model on a laptop, and it scores 11.8 on the test split, which we ran once at the very end. The trained systems lead on this task, and we say so plainly. They are classifiers built for this one benchmark; Pramana also routes the code, finds precedents, drafts arguments and explains its doubts. [click] It is strong on serious offences: murder at 0.78. Limitations
  • Zero-shot section choice trails trained systems (11.8 vs 28.1)
  • Minor companion offences often missed: 37% reach the candidate list
  • IPC→BNS mapping not yet reviewed by practising lawyers
  • Substantive criminal law only; English text; no scanned documents
Four challenges and how we handled them. There was no reliable machine-readable BNS, so we parsed the official gazette PDF ourselves. The embedder and the language model cannot share a 4 GB GPU, so every run hands the card from one to the other. The model invents sections, which the citation graph and the retry loop catch. And there is almost no BNS case law yet, which the IPC bridge solves. [click] The limitations: zero-shot section choice trails trained systems; minor companion offences are often missed; the mapping has not yet been reviewed by practising lawyers; and the scope is substantive criminal law in English text. Backup, for "why LangGraph" or "how do the stages talk to each other". The pipeline shares one typed state. Each stage writes only its own fields: the router writes the era, retrieval the candidates, Node 1 the proposals, Node 2 the verified and rejected sections and the retry count, and so on. Because each step's output is a field, each one can be checked independently, which a single prompt cannot offer. Three writers are the language model; the rest are code or graph lookups.
